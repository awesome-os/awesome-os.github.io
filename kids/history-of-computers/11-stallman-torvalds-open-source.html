CHAPTER 11 OF 12 · 1983–1991

Richard Stallman & Linus Torvalds

Free & Open-Source Software (GNU + Linux)
What They Invented

The Free Software movement (GNU, GPL) + the Linux kernel & Git version control

In the 1980s, big companies began locking software inside secret boxes so users couldn’t see how it worked or fix a broken line. Richard Stallman launched the **GNU Project**, arguing that every user deserves the freedom to read, study, share, and improve the software on their own computer.

In 1991, a 21-year-old university student in Finland named **Linus Torvalds** wrote a real operating system kernel and hardware drivers in **C** called **Linux**, talking directly to silicon while **GNU `libc`** stood as the clean doorway facing every outside program.

Thousands of programmers from every country joined in. Later, Linus also invented **Git** so developers could track every change mathematically using cryptographic hashes.

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Linux runs 100% of the world’s top 500 supercomputers, billions of Android phones, and most of the Internet’s servers — built in the open by people sharing knowledge.

Honest History Note

When humans share source code openly instead of hiding it behind walls, a single student’s hobby project can outgrow billion-dollar monopolies.
