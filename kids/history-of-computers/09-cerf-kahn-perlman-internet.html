CHAPTER 09 OF 12 · 1974–1985

Vint Cerf, Bob Kahn & Radia Perlman

TCP/IP & The Spanning Tree of the Internet
What They Invented

TCP/IP (how the Internet sends packets across Earth) + Spanning Tree Protocol (how networks avoid infinite loops)

Early computer networks were like isolated islands that couldn’t talk to each other. Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn invented **TCP/IP**: break every message into numbered postcards (packets), let routers pass them along any working path, and reassemble them at the destination even if a cable gets cut!

Meanwhile, connecting switches together had a sneaky trap: if wires formed a circle, packets would bounce around forever and crash the whole network. **Radia Perlman** solved this in a single weekend by inventing the **Spanning Tree Protocol (STP)** — a mathematical rule that automatically turns any messy web of cables into a safe, loop-free tree with instant backup paths.

Together, packet switching and loop-free routing turned isolated islands into one global Internet.

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Every single packet of video, chat, or game data that crosses your home router or undersea ocean cables uses TCP/IP and Radia Perlman’s routing ideas.

Honest History Note

Radia Perlman even wrote a playful poem ("Algorhyme") explaining her Spanning Tree algorithm — proof that deep math and joy belong together!
