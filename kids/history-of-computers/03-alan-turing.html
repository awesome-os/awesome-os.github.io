CHAPTER 03 OF 12 · 1936

Alan Turing

The Universal Machine
What They Invented

The Universal Turing Machine — proof that ONE programmable machine can run ANY program

Before 1936, people built one special machine for each job: one machine to add, another machine to weave cloth, another to predict tides.

Alan Turing asked a mind-blowing question: what is the simplest possible machine that can follow any set of rules? He imagined a tape of squares with symbols and a tiny read/write head moving left or right.

Then he proved something astonishing: you can build ONE "Universal Machine" that reads the blueprint of any other machine off the tape and pretends to be it! That is why one laptop can be a piano, a movie theater, a sketchbook, and a rocket simulator.

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Every CPU ever manufactured is a fast, practical cousin of Alan Turing’s Universal Machine.

Honest History Note

Turing also helped crack the Enigma cipher in World War II, saving millions of lives — and later suffered terrible injustice from his own government before finally being honored decades later.
