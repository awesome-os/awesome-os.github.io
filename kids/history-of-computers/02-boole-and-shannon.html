George Boole & Claude Shannon

True, False, and the Bit
What They Invented

Boolean logic (AND, OR, NOT) + electrical logic circuits + Information Theory (the Bit)

In 1854, George Boole showed that human logic — YES and NO, TRUE and FALSE — could be written as clean math using just three simple rules: AND, OR, and NOT.

Eighty-three years later, a 21-year-old student named Claude Shannon wrote the most famous master’s thesis of the century. He proved that ordinary electrical switches (ON = 1, OFF = 0) wired in series or parallel obey Boole’s exact math.

Later, Shannon invented Information Theory and popularized the word "Bit" (binary digit), proving mathematically how much information any wire or radio wave can carry without losing a single message.

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Every microchip has billions of tiny switches flipping 0 and 1 using Shannon’s circuit math — and every Wi-Fi, 5G, and ZIP file obeys Shannon’s information limit.

Honest History Note

Computers are not magic brains: they are billions of honest ON/OFF switches following Boole’s three simple rules.
