Ada Lovelace

The First Computer Programmer
What They Invented

The first published computer algorithm — and the vision of general-purpose computing

More than a hundred years before electric computers existed, a brilliant mathematician named Ada Lovelace studied Charles Babbage’s blueprint for a giant brass gear machine called the Analytical Engine.

Everyone else thought the machine was just a fancy calculator for adding taxes and star charts. Ada saw the truth: if numbers can represent letters, musical notes, or colors, then a machine following exact step-by-step rules can compose music, draw pictures, and solve puzzles.

In her famous Note G, she wrote out a step-by-step loop and variable table to compute Bernoulli numbers — the very first published computer program in human history.

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Every loop, variable, and program running on your phone, game console, or laptop follows the exact symbolic idea Ada Lovelace wrote down in 1843.

Honest History Note

The very first programmer in history was a woman — and she understood that computers only do what we know how to order them to perform.
