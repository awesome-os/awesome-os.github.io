CHAPTER 10 OF 12 · 1989–1991

Tim Berners-Lee

The World Wide Web — Given Away for Free
What They Invented

HTML, HTTP, URLs, the first web browser, and the first web server

The Internet was the road system, but in 1989 scientists still had to memorize cryptic commands just to fetch a file. At CERN in Switzerland, Tim Berners-Lee combined the Internet with hypertext links to create the **World Wide Web**.

He invented three simple, open standards: **URLs** (addresses), **HTTP** (how browsers ask servers for pages), and **HTML** (how pages link to other pages).

Most importantly of all: in 1993, Tim Berners-Lee and CERN put the World Wide Web into the **public domain for free** — no patent, no fee, no permission needed from any corporation to publish a website!

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

This very storybook you are reading right now is a plain HTML page served over HTTP — working the exact same way Tim Berners-Lee designed in 1991.

Honest History Note

If Tim Berners-Lee had patented the Web instead of giving it to humanity for free, nobody could build a website today without paying a toll.
