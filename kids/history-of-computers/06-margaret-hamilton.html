CHAPTER 06 OF 12 · 1969

Margaret Hamilton

Software Engineering & Safe Gates to the Moon
What They Invented

Software Engineering as a discipline + fault-tolerant priority recovery software for Apollo 11

When NASA planned to land humans on the Moon, people didn’t even think software deserved to be called "engineering." Margaret Hamilton led the team at MIT that wrote the flight software for Apollo 11 — and coined the term "Software Engineering."

Three minutes before Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin touched down on the Moon, a radar switch was accidentally left in the wrong position, flooding the tiny flight computer with extra data (Alarm 1202).

Because Margaret Hamilton had insisted on building a **Safe Gate** — a priority-recovery system that automatically dropped low-priority tasks and kept the landing thrusters running — the computer recovered in milliseconds and landed Apollo 11 safely on the Moon!

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Every spacecraft, airplane, hospital monitor, and self-checking Safe Gate in Awesome-OS owes its life to Margaret Hamilton’s fault-tolerant design.

Honest History Note

The famous photo of Margaret Hamilton standing next to a stack of Apollo code printouts taller than herself proves how carefully humans had to verify every single line before launch.
