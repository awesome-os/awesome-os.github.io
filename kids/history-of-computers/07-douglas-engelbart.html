CHAPTER 07 OF 12 · 1968

Douglas Engelbart

The Mother of All Demos
What They Invented

The computer mouse, hypertext links, windows, video calls, and live shared documents

In 1968, most computers didn’t even have screens — they printed answers onto rolls of paper! Then Douglas Engelbart walked onto a stage in San Francisco and gave a 90-minute live demonstration that left a thousand engineers speechless.

In a single afternoon, he unveiled the very first wooden computer mouse, clickable hypertext links, split-screen windows, live video calling, and two people editing the same document over a network at the same time.

His goal wasn’t to replace humans with machines — it was to "augment human intellect" so small teams could solve huge problems together.

Why It Still Runs Inside Your Device Today

Every time you move a mouse, click a link, open a window, or join a video call, you are using Douglas Engelbart’s 1968 vision.

Honest History Note

Engelbart and his teammate Bill English built the first mouse out of a carved block of wood with two metal wheels inside!
