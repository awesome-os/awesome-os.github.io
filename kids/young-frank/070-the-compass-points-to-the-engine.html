Chapter 70: The Compass Points to the Engine

An epiphany is a spark, not yet a bridge. Once the thrill of that morning settled into quiet determination, Frank asked the next, hardest engineering question: Where do you even begin to unify a mountain?

He knew better than to disappear into a cave for twenty years trying to invent every single transistor, compiler, and network driver from scratch. That was the trap of pure theory—beautiful on a chalkboard, useless in the real world where billions of people already lived, worked, and created. To unify the mountain for real, he needed a foundation that already possessed brutal, battle-tested speed, relentless security engineering, and a footprint that reached nearly every screen on Earth.

When he turned his monitors back on, the open-source landscape looked completely different. He was no longer shopping for shiny frameworks, trendy starter kits, or clever developer wrappers. His eyes went straight past the surface noise and locked onto the deep engines—the rare pieces of open-source iron that actually moved the modern world.

He looked at the operating system kernels. He looked at the compilers. And most of all, he looked at the browser engines—those staggering, misunderstood masterpieces of systems engineering that sat quietly on billions of phones, laptops, and desktops, already executing untrusted code safely at near-native speed across every CPU and operating system on the planet. While much of the industry treated the browser as a mere document viewer or a toy sandbox, Frank saw something far bigger hiding in plain sight: a universal, cross-platform virtual machine forged in the fire of the open web.

He folded the ink drawing of the mountain carefully and taped it to the wall beside his desk, right where he would see it every time he reached for the keyboard. Beneath it, he opened a fresh workspace—not to clone another hundred random packages, but to read the actual source code of the world's most powerful JavaScript and browser engines with his own eyes.

Other engineers would soon try to steer him toward flashier alternatives, newer hype cycles, and louder promises. Frank already knew he wouldn't listen to pitch decks anymore. The Open-Source Odyssey had burned away every illusion. From this day forward, he would trust only what he could verify in the code itself—and the compass was pointing straight toward the engine room.

Frank's Memory Note

You don't unify a fractured world by chasing the loudest hype; you find the strongest engine already forged in fire and read its source code line by line.
