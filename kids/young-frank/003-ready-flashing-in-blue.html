Chapter 3: READY. Flashing in Blue

If the Atari was a locked workshop with a narrow window, the Commodore 64 was the front door thrown wide open. It arrived on Frank's desk as a rounded, warm-beige wedge—the famous "breadbin"—with a thick, sculpted keyboard whose tall brown keys demanded a confident press. Unlike the Atari, it had no cartridge waiting in the slot when Frank flipped the power switch on the right side. Instead, the CRT flickered and bloomed into an unforgettable frame: a wide border of soft periwinkle blue surrounding a deep royal-blue sea.

At the top of the screen sat three crisp lines of pale blue text: * COMMODORE 64 BASIC V2 *, followed by 64K RAM SYSTEM 38911 BASIC BYTES FREE, and finally a single word that changed the trajectory of Frank's life: READY. Beneath it, a solid blue square blinked steadily, twice every second, waiting with infinite patience for him to speak.

At first, the blinking cursor felt intimidating. A game cartridge told you what to do the instant it booted, throwing enemies at you before you could even blink. The Commodore 64 offered nothing except a blank stage and a quiet invitation. Frank opened the spiral-bound User's Guide that smelled of fresh ink and stiff cardboard, turning to the first pages where simple numbered lines of BASIC were printed in bold monospace type.

With two careful index fingers, he hunted across the clacking keys and typed:

10 PRINT "FRANK ";

20 GOTO 10

He pressed RETURN, took a slow breath, and typed RUN.

The screen exploded into motion. His own name cascaded diagonally down the blue glass in an endless, shimmering waterfall, scrolling faster than his eyes could track. Frank gasped and laughed out loud, his heart hammering against his ribs. For the first time in his life, the television was not showing him a broadcast from a distant studio or a game designed by strangers in California. It was obeying him—line by line, character by character, with total loyalty.

He hit the RUN/STOP key, and the cascade froze instantly with a polite BREAK IN 10 and another steady READY. cursor. Immediately he began experimenting, adding color control codes with CTRL and the number keys, changing the message, making the computer ask his name with INPUT and greet him back. Hours dissolved like mist. When his mother called him for dinner, Frank realized his hands were still resting on the warm beige plastic as if it were a musical instrument he had only just discovered how to play. The cursor kept blinking in the dusk, patient and honest, waiting for his next idea.

Frank's Memory Note

A blinking READY. cursor is the purest invitation ever invented: it does not care how old you are, only what you dare to type next.
