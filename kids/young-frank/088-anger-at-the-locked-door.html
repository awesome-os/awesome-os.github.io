Chapter 88: Anger at the Locked Door

At two in the morning, the calm finally snapped. Another thread—one Frank had poured three weeks of measurements, diagrams, and bare-metal traces into—had been locked with a three-line moderator note calling the scope unactionable. Something hot and heavy surged up from his chest, sharp as winter static. He slammed the laptop lid shut so hard the desk lamp rattled, pushed his chair back, and walked straight out into the cold night air without even grabbing a jacket.

His breath hung in pale clouds under the streetlamp as he paced the empty sidewalk. He was angry—genuinely, fiercely angry, in a way he hadn't felt since his twenties. Not the petty anger of a bruised ego, and not even hatred toward the exhausted engineers on the other side of those screens. It was a deeper, aching fury at the sheer human waste of it all: millions of hours of brilliant developers' lives burned wrestling broken builds, gigawatts of global electricity wasted on bloated abstraction layers, and a whole generation of young builders being taught that slow, fragile software was just the natural law of the universe.

"Why won't anyone just look at the whole picture?" he muttered to the dark street, his fists jammed deep into his pockets. His heart hammered against his ribs. For a bitter, dangerous minute, he wondered why he even bothered trying to share what he saw. Other people took the easy road, cashed the checks, smiled at the conferences, and never lost a night's sleep over a three-second startup delay. Why was he tearing himself apart knocking on doors that had bolted themselves from the inside?

When he finally came back through the front door, cheeks red from the cold, the warm yellow light of the kitchen spilled into the hallway. She was standing by the counter in an oversized sweater, two steaming mugs of tea already waiting, having heard the front latch click. She didn't lecture him about committees or tell him to calm down. She just stepped close, wrapped her warm hands around his freezing fingers, and rested her forehead quietly against his shoulder until his breathing slowed.

"You're allowed to be angry, Frank," she said softly, handing him a mug. "Just don't let a locked door turn your fire into rust." Sitting at the kitchen table in the quiet glow of 2:30 AM, feeling the warmth seep back into his hands, Frank realized she was right. Anger could wake you up, and love could keep you human—but bitterness was a trap that never built a single line of honest code.

Frank's Memory Note

Righteous frustration can fuel a builder for a mile, but only warmth and clarity can carry you across the whole journey.
