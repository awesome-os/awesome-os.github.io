Chapter 80: Drawing the Blueprint for the Standards Fight

Having the blueprint in your notebook was one thing. Getting the rest of the software world to see it was another story entirely.

Frank knew how ecosystems worked—he had spent the last year reading their public meeting minutes, RFCs, and mailing lists. A lone builder coding in a dark room could prove a concept, even build a breathtaking engine, but if the language specifications, module loaders, runtime standards, and developer editors remained fractured into competing fiefdoms, every developer on Earth would stay trapped in the same glue-code swamp.

To truly unify the mountain, you couldn't just build on the sidelines. You had to walk straight into the halls where the rules were written.

He looked at the list of names he had taped to the edge of his monitor: ECMA (where JavaScript's future was decided), TypeScript (the language half the world actually wrote), WinterCG (the group trying to align web-interoperable server runtimes), the Node.js package maintenance teams, and the sprawling galaxy of VS Code editors and forks. Each one held a vital piece of the puzzle. Each one also had its own committees, its own habits, its own fiercely guarded boundaries.

Frank wasn't naive. He had watched enough public governance threads to know that walking into established standards bodies with a holistic, cross-layer vision was like trying to hand a complete symphony score to five orchestras that were each only tuned to play their own measure. People got defensive. Silos protected themselves. Sometimes the hardest wall in computer science wasn't a hardware limit or a compiler bug—it was getting two rooms full of brilliant people to look past their own corner of the map.

Before shutting down his machine for the night, he saved his architecture notes, checked his references one last time, and looked out the window at the dark, quiet street. The Atari glow from his childhood felt both a lifetime away and right beside his shoulder—a quiet reminder that curiosity was never meant to stay small or obedient.

He took a deep breath, opened a blank draft to the standards working groups, and began to type. The study phase was over. Now came the crusade.

Frank's Memory Note

When the blueprint is finally clear in your hands, silence is no longer an option—you step into the room where the standards are written and make your case.
